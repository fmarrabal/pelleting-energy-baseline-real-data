\begin{abstract}
Assessing electricity use in feed pelleting requires a baseline that accounts for what each production line processes. We studied 2,745 real operational records from three lines, using dosed mass, batch count, product and machine characteristics, and calendar information to estimate pelleting electricity in kWh per record. Seven methods were first compared on 800 chronological development records. The selected Ridge baseline reduced mean absolute error from 61.29 to 52.45 kWh in a subsequent 798-record test, a 14.42\% improvement over machine-specific energy intensity. We then examined whether completed operating histories added predictive value. On 584 matched development targets with 16-record contexts, GPU-trained Temporal Fusion Transformer achieved MAE 53.65 kWh, compared with 53.55 kWh for the corresponding Ridge control. The paired confidence interval did not establish an improvement; N-HiTS and the tested TimesFM 2.5 and Chronos-2 variants also performed worse than the control. Ridge's additive structure made individual estimates directly interpretable, although substantial underestimation remained on Pellet~1. Fixed 90\% and 95\% intervals covered 85.96\% and 93.23\% of test records. Updating calibration increased coverage mainly by widening the intervals. These findings support a transparent production-adjusted baseline for retrospective assessment. Prospective forecasting will require confirmation of input availability and measurement boundaries, followed by evaluation on a new industrial period.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} feed pelleting; electrical energy; production-adjusted baseline; temporal models; explainable regression; prediction intervals.

\section{Conclusions}
A Ridge model accounting for production conditions reduced prediction error by 14.42\% against a machine-specific energy-intensity reference in the original chronological test. Its transparent structure made the estimated contribution of each input accessible, while the residuals revealed persistent differences between machines. The subsequent comparison on matched 16-record histories did not establish an advantage for TFT, N-HiTS or the tested TimesFM 2.5 and Chronos-2 variants. Product-dependent mass effects and recent-residual correction offered modest exploratory improvements, whereas updated uncertainty calibration mainly produced wider intervals. The frozen Ridge model therefore remains a useful reference for retrospective energy assessment and for a future digital-twin interface. Its operational value now depends on establishing when inputs and outcomes become available and testing the selected policies on a new industrial period.
