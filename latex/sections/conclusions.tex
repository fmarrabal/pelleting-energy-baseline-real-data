\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
On 798 real pelleting records evaluated after the model had been frozen, a production-adjusted Ridge baseline reduced MAE by 14.42\% relative to the line-specific SEC reference that plants use in practice, on all three lines, while every estimate remained exactly decomposable into its inputs. The gain is in the estimation of expected electricity under the recorded production conditions; it says nothing about plant energy use having fallen or about savings that could be achieved.

Giving the models a history of completed operations did not help. Under the tested representation of 16 same-line operations within the week, neither the history-enabled Ridge control nor TFT, N-HiTS or the tested TimesFM~2.5 and Chronos-2 configurations beat the current-input control on identical records, and the two pretrained pipelines showed instabilities on irregular industrial contexts (an extrapolating covariate adapter in one case, a device-dependent normalization in the other) that had more to do with covariate handling than with the pretrained weights. Production adjustment worked most consistently on Pellet~2 and Pellet~3; Pellet~1 improved on SEC but remained systematically underestimated and needs line-specific recalibration or additional predictors before any prospective use.

What the evidence supports today is retrospective assessment at the closure of each recorded operation: an expected value, an empirically calibrated residual interval and a traceable deviation that marks candidates for technical review. Prospective supervisory use will require validation on a new consecutive production period with prespecified line-level criteria, together with verified record identity, measurement boundaries, predictor availability and calibration rules. Until then, automatic control, modification of process set-points and operational digital-twin integration remain outside the demonstrated scope.
