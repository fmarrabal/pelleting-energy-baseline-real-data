\section{Materials and methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Study design}
This is a retrospective observational model-development study on recorded operations of three industrial feed-pelleting lines. It estimates the expected electricity of completed operations rather than forecasting a continuously sampled power signal, and its observational unit is an operation represented by one row of the plant database. The primary evaluation develops and selects models on temporally ordered validation periods, freezes the selected model and evaluates it against operational reference baselines in a later test period, and assesses predictive performance and interval coverage overall and by line. Complementary analyses examine short histories of completed same-line operations with locally trained temporal architectures and pretrained time-series models, line- and product-specific mass interactions, a recent-residual correction, rolling updates of the interval widths, and numerical agreement between computational implementations. None of them replaces the frozen model. Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarizes the workflow.

\subsection{Industrial records and observational unit}
The plant workbook holds 2,745 records and 32 named variables from three lines: 730 from Pellet~1, 1,148 from Pellet~2 and 867 from Pellet~3, with recorded stage starts from 2 January to 1 April 2025 and the last pelleting completion on 2 April. The plant information system generated each row as a consolidated record of one production operation: dosed mass, dosing-batch count, product classifications, stage timestamps, pelleting electrical energy (\texttt{KWH PELET}), steam-related variables and operational summaries. There is no verified production-order identifier linking every stage to a single physical batch and no separate meter history from which the energy value could be reconstructed, so a ``recorded production operation'' is one row of the workbook, and source-row identifiers are preserved throughout. Records with overlapping timestamps were kept when their identifiers differed, because overlap alone is no evidence of duplication; overlaps are characterized by line and treated as a data-quality descriptor.

\subsection{Target, predictors and preprocessing}
The response $y_i$ is \texttt{KWH PELET}, the electrical energy attributed to pelleting record $i$ in kWh; it excludes the separately recorded electricity of milling and mixing and the thermal energy supplied as steam. Dosed mass $m_i$ comes from \texttt{PESO DOS (kg)} converted to tonnes. Energy, a duration-based average-power proxy and specific energy consumption are defined as
\begin{equation}
 E_i=y_i\;[\mathrm{kWh}],\qquad
 P_i^{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{y_i}{\Delta_i}\;[\mathrm{kW}],\qquad
 \mathrm{SEC}_i=\frac{y_i}{m_i}\;[\mathrm{kWh/t}],
 \label{eq:units}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is the recorded pelleting duration in hours. $P_i^{\mathrm{avg}}$ is derived, not measured; $E_i$ is the modelling target and SEC is used only to build the reference baselines.

The analytical origin $t_i$ is the earliest recorded start among milling, mixing and pelleting; it assigns records to partitions and decides whether a historical outcome was completed before a target. The predictors are dosed mass, dosing-batch count, pelleting line, product subfamily, presentation, bag/bulk category, origin hour and origin weekday (Table~\ref{tab:inputs}). With $b_i$ the number of dosing batches, $h_i$ the origin hour and $d_i\in\{0,\ldots,6\}$ the weekday from Monday, the numerical input vector is
\begin{equation}
 x_i^{\mathrm{num}}=\left(m_i,b_i,\sin\frac{2\pi h_i}{24},\cos\frac{2\pi h_i}{24},
 \sin\frac{2\pi d_i}{7},\cos\frac{2\pi d_i}{7}\right).
 \label{eq:inputs}
\end{equation}

The six numerical coordinates are standardized with training-data parameters only and the four categorical variables are one-hot encoded with training vocabularies, giving 22 columns in the Ridge design matrix; a level absent from a training period produces no active indicator. Grouped Ridge contributions therefore depend partly on this coding and are read as components of the fitted representation, not as invariant physical effects. The analysis is retrospective and conditional on the consolidated production characteristics of each operation: current-record durations, stage energies and the steam, temperature and current summaries are excluded as inputs because their availability at the origin cannot be established, and recorded pelleting completion is taken as the availability time of historical outcomes. A prospective implementation would have to restrict the model to descriptors verified as available at the intended origin.

\subsection{Data integrity and temporal consistency}
All 32 source fields were reconciled with the analytical table across the 2,745 records: the 87,840 cell-level comparisons satisfied the predefined numerical and whitespace tolerances. Three records (0.11\%) with \texttt{KWH PELET}=0 were excluded from supervised modelling, without imputation. Table~\ref{tab:quality} lists the main checks; the counts describe different characteristics of possibly the same records. Median pelleting durations are about 145, 96 and 123 minutes on Pellet~1, 2 and 3; milling completion falls after pelleting start in 528 records and mixing completion in 376. These patterns are characteristics of consolidated plant records rather than exclusion criteria. The workbook contains event-level summaries, not dense power trajectories or verified active/inactive labels; a separately supplied set of synthetic ten-second series was kept apart from the industrial evidence.

\subsection{Chronological evaluation design}
Records are assigned by origin to development (before 22 February 2025), calibration (22 February to 5 March) and testing (from 6 March); the last operation completes on 2 April (Figure~\ref{fig:allocation}). Development and calibration eligibility require a positive outcome, a valid duration, complete inputs and completion before the next boundary, which retains 1,542 of 1,556 development candidates and 380 of 391 calibration candidates (the 25 exclusions of Table~\ref{tab:quality}); all 798 test candidates are retained, including the five operations longer than 24 hours.

\fig{01_records_and_partitions}{allocation}{1}{Chronological allocation by pelleting line and partition. (a) Daily record counts for the three lines; background shading marks the development, calibration and frozen-test partitions, and dashed lines mark the development--calibration boundary on 22 February 2025 and the start of testing on 6 March 2025. Each record is counted by its earliest recorded stage start. (b) Dates, candidate records, exclusions and retained sample sizes of each partition.}

Model development uses four expanding training blocks with validation weeks beginning on 25 January, 1 February, 8 February and 15 February 2025: training sizes 705, 924, 1,121 and 1,334, validation sizes 208, 187, 205 and 200, i.e.\ 800 common targets over 28 origin dates. Training outcomes must be completed before each validation period starts, and validation outcomes before the week ends, so that the expanding blocks remain separated. Assessment thus follows the direction in which the model would be used and keeps future outcomes out of both model fitting and historical contexts \citep{tashman2000,cerqueira2020,bergmeir2018,kapoor2023}.

\subsection{Reference baselines and current-input models}
With $\ell_i$ the line of operation $i$, the line-specific SEC reference estimates one energy intensity per line from training totals:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{s}_\ell=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T:\ell_i=\ell}y_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T:\ell_i=\ell}m_i},
 \qquad \widehat y_i^{\mathrm{SEC}}=m_i\widehat s_{\ell_i}.
 \label{eq:sec}
\end{equation}
A line-specific median-energy reference and a recent-SEC reference (median of the last five completed same-line energy-to-mass ratios, updated as outcomes arrive during validation) complete the set of operational references. With $z_i$ the transformed current-record inputs, Ridge regression estimates an unpenalized intercept and regularized coefficients,
\begin{equation}
 (\widehat b,\widehat\beta)=\argmin_{b,\beta}
 \left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}(y_i-b-z_i^\top\beta)^2+10\|\beta\|_2^2\right\},
 \qquad \widehat y_i=\max(0,\widehat b+z_i^\top\widehat\beta),
 \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
where the non-negative truncation prevents negative energy estimates without imposing a process-energy balance. ExtraTrees (250 trees, minimum leaf size five), histogram gradient boosting (200 iterations, seven leaves, learning rate 0.05, minimum leaf size 20, regularization 10) and a multilayer perceptron (hidden layers of 32 and 16 units, Adam, regularization 0.1, at most 800 iterations, seeds 11, 29 and 47 averaged) use the same inputs with configurations held constant across validation blocks; the tree methods use seed 2026. These are the implementations evaluated here, not an exhaustive optimization of each family. When Ridge and gradient boosting gave practically the same development MAE, Ridge was chosen for parsimony, stability and record-level traceability. The selected model and its interval calibration were frozen before the test records were evaluated.

For interpretation, the 22 design columns are assigned to eight groups (dosed mass, batch count, hour, weekday, line, subfamily, presentation, bag/bulk), so that the untruncated estimate and the contribution of group $g$ are
\begin{equation}
 f_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}=\widehat b+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}C_{ig},\qquad
 C_{ig}=\sum_{k\in g}z_{ik}\widehat\beta_k,\qquad
 \widehat y_i=\max(0,f_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}).
 \label{eq:explain}
\end{equation}
The grouped contributions preserve the arithmetic of the fitted model; they describe how the encoded variables build an estimate, not separate physical energy uses or causal effects \citep{rudin2019,aas2021,fisher2019}.

\subsection{Short-context temporal models on irregular operational events}
The temporal comparison asks whether histories of completed same-line operations add information beyond the current-record covariates. Each eligible target receives the 16 most recent completed records from the same line and validation interval,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_i^{(16)}=\operatorname{last}_{16,(a_j,j)}
 \{j:\ell_j=\ell_i,\ t_j\in\mathcal P,\ a_j<t_i,\ a_j<\sup\mathcal P\},
 \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
where $a_j$ is the recorded completion time and ties are broken by source row; an operation that started earlier but was not complete at the target origin cannot enter the context. The window retains 584 of the 800 development targets (149, 257 and 178 from Pellet~1, 2 and 3 over 21 origin dates). Contexts restart at the weekly validation boundaries; completed outcomes may inform later targets within the week but never update fitted parameters. Figure~\ref{fig:windows} shows the design and one observed context.

\fig{07_windows}{windows}{1}{Short-context temporal evaluation. (a) Expanding training blocks and weekly validation blocks; green bars mark the outer validation weeks, and each training bar reports the earlier training / preceding-week validation counts used for epoch selection. (b) An observed context of 16 completed same-line operations for target row 823 on Pellet~1, ordered by completion; segments span recorded pelleting start and finish, dots mark completion, and every donor outcome precedes the target origin on 28 January at 04:24.}

Two Ridge controls separate historical information from model capacity: one uses the current covariates only, the other appends the 16 historical covariate vectors and their energies, keeping the penalty of 10. TFT and N-HiTS receive the same information through their NeuralForecast implementations \citep{nixtlatft,nixtlanhits}; TFT uses hidden size 32, four heads and dropout 0.1, N-HiTS three blocks of two 64-unit layers with pooling 2, 2, 1. Each uses seeds 11, 29 and 47 with an inner validation week before each outer week for epoch selection (Adam at $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 64, gradient clipping 1, at most 40 epochs, patience 8); each seed is refitted on the full outer-training block for its selected epochs and the three clipped predictions are averaged. TimesFM~2.5 runs zero-shot with its frozen 200M checkpoint, alone and with its XReg adapter (ridge 10, one call per origin so that no query pools labels from another query's future) \citep{das2024,timesfm25,timesfmrepo}; Chronos-2 runs with energy-only and native covariate inputs, median forecast, cross-query learning disabled \citep{ansari2025,chronosrepo}. All variants are evaluated on the same 584 targets, so the comparison concerns these short-context implementations on irregular pelleting events, not the model families in general \citep{aksu2024,zeng2023}.

\subsection{Post-hoc extensions}
Three extensions turn patterns seen during development into hypotheses for later periods. H1 lets the mass association vary by line and H2 by product subfamily through the untruncated predictor
\begin{equation}
 f_i=b+z_i^\top\beta+z_{m,i}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\gamma_c\ind(g_i=c),
 \label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
with grouping variable $g_i$, the same penalty and training-only transformations. H3 corrects the frozen estimate with up to 20 recent same-line residuals $e_j=y_j-\widehat y_j^{\mathrm{Ridge}}$ of completed operations, ordered by completion and source row; with $n_i=|\mathcal H_i|$,
\begin{equation}
 \delta_i=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{n_i}{n_i+10}\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{j\in\mathcal H_i}e_j,&n_i\ge5,\\
 0,&n_i<5,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \widehat y_i^{\mathrm{H3}}=\max(0,\widehat y_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}+\delta_i).
 \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
H1--H3 are exploratory development analyses; they do not replace the frozen model.

\subsection{Residual intervals and performance metrics}
Absolute errors on the 380 calibration records calibrate the intervals of the frozen model. For miscoverage level $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
 q_\alpha=r_{(\lceil(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)},\qquad
 r_j=|y_j-\widehat y_j|,\qquad
 I_i^{1-\alpha}=[\max(0,\widehat y_i-q_\alpha),\widehat y_i+q_\alpha],
 \label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
giving half-widths of 93.01 and 132.96~kWh at the 90\% and 95\% levels. They are called residual intervals because the industrial sequence is not assumed exchangeable; they are judged empirically by coverage, mean width and interval score, globally and by line. A post-hoc rolling policy recomputes the quantile from the latest 200 available absolute errors, starting from the calibration residuals and admitting a test residual only once the completion of its operation precedes the target origin ($a_j<t_i$); it is an exploratory comparator, not a validated improvement \citep{xu2021,zaffran2022,gibbs2024}.

Point performance is measured by
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{MAE}&=N^{-1}\sum_i|\widehat y_i-y_i|,&
 \mathrm{RMSE}&=\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i(\widehat y_i-y_i)^2},\\
 \mathrm{Bias}&=N^{-1}\sum_i(\widehat y_i-y_i),&
 \mathrm{WAPE}&=\frac{\sum_i|\widehat y_i-y_i|}{\sum_i y_i},
\end{align}
with MAE as the selection criterion \citep{hyndman2006}. Intervals are assessed by coverage, mean width and the interval score \citep{gneiting2007},
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{IS}_\alpha(L,U;y)=(U-L)+\frac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)\ind(y<L)
                  +\frac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)\ind(y>U),
 \label{eq:is}
\end{equation}
and each completed operation yields a signed excess $r_i^{\mathrm{excess}}=y_i-\widehat y_i$ and an out-of-interval flag $B_i=\ind\{y_i<L_i\ \text{or}\ y_i>U_i\}$ that mark candidates for review without identifying the physical cause. Paired differences use a circular block bootstrap over observed origin dates (blocks of three dates, all lines of a date together; 3,000 replicates for the test comparison, 5,000 for the exploratory ones), with Bonferroni-adjusted 98.33\% intervals for H1--H3 and 99.29\% intervals for the seven temporal contrasts alongside the marginal 95\% intervals.

\subsection{Computational implementation}
Ridge and the native JAX XReg solver ran on CPU; neural computations used float32 on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell GPU (48~GB) with TF32 disabled. TFT and N-HiTS were trained locally, whereas TimesFM and Chronos weights remained frozen. CPU--GPU numerical comparisons are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:computing}.
