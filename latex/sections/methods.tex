\section{Materials and methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Study design}
This is a retrospective observational model-development study. It uses recorded operations from three industrial feed-pelleting lines to build and evaluate production-adjusted baselines for electrical energy, and it estimates the expected energy of completed operations rather than forecasting a continuously sampled power signal. The observational unit is an operation represented by one row of the plant database.

The primary evaluation develops and selects models on temporally ordered validation periods, evaluates the selected model, frozen, against operational reference baselines in an independent chronological test period, and assesses predictive performance and residual-interval coverage overall and by pelleting line. Complementary analyses examine the predictive value of short histories of completed same-line operations, locally trained temporal architectures and pretrained time-series models, line- and product-specific mass interactions, a recent-residual correction, rolling updates of the interval widths, and numerical agreement between computational implementations. These analyses characterize alternative modelling hypotheses; none of them replaces the model frozen for the primary test. Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarizes the workflow.

\subsection{Industrial data source and observational unit}
The source workbook holds 2,745 records and 32 named variables from three feed-pelleting lines: 730 from Pellet~1, 1,148 from Pellet~2 and 867 from Pellet~3. Recorded stage starts run from 2 January to 1 April 2025, and the last pelleting operation was completed on 2 April 2025. The plant information system generated each row as a consolidated record of one production operation, with dosed mass, dosing-batch count, product classifications, stage timestamps, pelleting electrical energy, steam-related variables and operational summaries; the field \texttt{KWH PELET} is the energy value the system assigned to that row.

The database contains no verified production-order identifier linking every recorded stage to a single physical batch, and no separate history of meter readings from which the row-level energy could be reconstructed. A ``recorded production operation'' is therefore one row of the workbook, not an independently verified physical batch, and source-row identifiers are preserved throughout for traceability. Records with overlapping timestamps were kept when they had distinct identifiers, because overlap alone is not evidence of duplication; overlaps were characterized by line and treated as a data-quality descriptor when interpreting the chronological analyses.

\subsection{Target variable, predictors and preprocessing}
The response $y_i$ is \texttt{KWH PELET}, the electrical energy attributed to pelleting record $i$ in kWh. It excludes the separately recorded electricity of milling and mixing and the thermal energy supplied as steam. Dosed mass $m_i$ comes from \texttt{PESO DOS (kg)} converted to tonnes. Energy, a duration-based average-power proxy and specific energy consumption are defined as
\begin{equation}
 E_i=y_i\;[\mathrm{kWh}],\qquad
 P_i^{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{y_i}{\Delta_i}\;[\mathrm{kW}],\qquad
 \mathrm{SEC}_i=\frac{y_i}{m_i}\;[\mathrm{kWh/t}],
 \label{eq:units}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is the recorded pelleting duration in hours. $P_i^{\mathrm{avg}}$ is derived from recorded energy and duration and is not a power measurement. $E_i$ is the modelling target; SEC is used only to construct the operational reference baselines.

The analytical origin $t_i$ is the earliest recorded start among milling, mixing and pelleting. It assigns records to chronological partitions and decides whether a historical outcome was completed before a target. The current-record predictors are dosed mass, dosing-batch count, pelleting-line identity, product subfamily, presentation, bag/bulk category, origin hour and origin weekday (Table~\ref{tab:inputs}). With $b_i$ the number of dosing batches, $h_i$ the origin hour and $d_i\in\{0,\ldots,6\}$ the weekday indexed from Monday, the numerical input vector is
\begin{equation}
 x_i^{\mathrm{num}}=\left(m_i,b_i,\sin\frac{2\pi h_i}{24},\cos\frac{2\pi h_i}{24},
 \sin\frac{2\pi d_i}{7},\cos\frac{2\pi d_i}{7}\right).
 \label{eq:inputs}
\end{equation}

The six numerical coordinates are standardized with parameters estimated from the corresponding training data only, and the four categorical variables are one-hot encoded with training vocabularies, giving 22 columns in the Ridge design matrix. A category absent from a training period produces no active indicator in its field. Because grouped Ridge contributions depend partly on this coding, they are read as components of the fitted representation, not as invariant physical effects.

The primary analysis is retrospective and conditional on the consolidated production characteristics recorded for each operation. Current-record durations, stage-specific energy values and the complete steam, temperature and electrical-current summaries are excluded from the predictors. For historical outcomes, recorded pelleting completion is taken as the availability time. A prospective implementation would have to restrict the model to descriptors verified as available at the intended prediction origin.

\subsection{Data integrity and temporal consistency}
All 32 source fields were reconciled with the analytical table across the 2,745 records; the 87,840 cell-level comparisons satisfied the predefined numerical and whitespace tolerances, confirming that the analytical copy matches the workbook. Three records (0.11\%) with \texttt{KWH PELET}=0 were excluded from supervised modelling because they provide no positive target; their cause could not be established independently and no targets were imputed. Median recorded pelleting durations are about 145, 96 and 123 minutes on Pellet~1, Pellet~2 and Pellet~3. Milling completion falls after pelleting start in 528 records and mixing completion in 376, and the two sets overlap. \subsection{Chronological evaluation design}
Records are assigned by analytical origin to development (origins before 22 February 2025), calibration (22 February to 5 March 2025) and testing (from 6 March 2025); the last operation completes on 2 April 2025 (Figure~\ref{fig:allocation}). Development and calibration eligibility require a positive recorded outcome, a valid duration, complete required inputs and completion before the next partition boundary, which retains 1,542 of 1,556 development candidates and 380 of 391 calibration candidates; the 14 and 11 exclusions are the 25 pre-test exclusions of Table~\ref{tab:quality}. All 798 test candidates are retained, including the five records longer than 24 hours.

\fig{01_records_and_partitions}{allocation}{1}{Chronological allocation by pelleting line and partition. (a) Daily record counts for the three lines; background shading marks the development, calibration and frozen-test partitions, and dashed lines mark the development--calibration boundary on 22 February 2025 and the start of testing on 6 March 2025. Each record is counted by its earliest recorded stage start. (b) Dates, candidate records, exclusions and retained sample sizes of each partition.}

Model development uses four expanding training blocks with validation weeks beginning on 25 January, 1 February, 8 February and 15 February 2025. Training sizes are 705, 924, 1,121 and 1,334 records and validation sizes 208, 187, 205 and 200, giving 800 common development targets over 28 observed origin dates. Training outcomes must be completed before each validation period starts, and validation outcomes before the corresponding week ends, which keeps the expanding blocks separated. Assessment thus follows the temporal direction of model use and keeps future outcomes out of both model fitting and historical contexts \citep{tashman2000,cerqueira2020,bergmeir2018,kapoor2023}.

\subsection{Reference baselines and current-input models}
Three simple operational references were included. With $\ell_i$ the pelleting line of operation $i$, the line-specific SEC reference estimates one energy intensity per line from training totals:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{s}_\ell=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T:\ell_i=\ell}y_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T:\ell_i=\ell}m_i},
 \qquad \widehat y_i^{\mathrm{SEC}}=m_i\widehat s_{\ell_i}.
 \label{eq:sec}
\end{equation}
A line-specific median-energy reference and a recent-SEC reference, the median of the last five completed same-line energy-to-mass ratios, complete the set. The recent reference updates as outcomes become available during validation; the static references and the fitted models keep their parameters.

Let $z_i$ be the transformed current-record inputs. Ridge regression estimates an unpenalized intercept and regularized coefficients,
\begin{equation}
 (\widehat b,\widehat\beta)=\argmin_{b,\beta}
 \left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}(y_i-b-z_i^\top\beta)^2+10\|\beta\|_2^2\right\},
 \qquad \widehat y_i=\max(0,\widehat b+z_i^\top\widehat\beta).
 \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
The non-negative truncation prevents negative energy estimates without imposing a complete process-energy balance.

ExtraTrees, histogram gradient boosting and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) use the same current-record inputs with fixed configurations: 250 trees with a minimum leaf size of five for ExtraTrees; 200 boosting iterations, seven leaves, a learning rate of 0.05, a minimum leaf size of 20 and regularization of 10 for gradient boosting; and hidden layers of 32 and 16 units, Adam optimization, regularization of 0.1 and at most 800 iterations for the MLP. Neural predictions average seeds 11, 29 and 47; the tree methods use seed 2026. The configurations were held constant across validation blocks and represent the implementations evaluated here, not an exhaustive optimization of each family. When Ridge and gradient boosting gave practically the same development MAE, Ridge was chosen for parsimony, stability and record-level traceability rather than for predictive superiority. The selected Ridge model and its interval calibration were frozen before the 798 test records were evaluated.

For interpretation, the 22 design columns are assigned to eight groups: dosed mass, dosing-batch count, origin hour, origin weekday, pelleting line, product subfamily, presentation and bag/bulk category. The untruncated estimate and the contribution of group $g$ are
\begin{equation}
 f_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}=\widehat b+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}C_{ig},\qquad
 C_{ig}=\sum_{k\in g}z_{ik}\widehat\beta_k,\qquad
 \widehat y_i=\max(0,f_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}).
 \label{eq:explain}
\end{equation}
The grouped contributions preserve the arithmetic of the fitted model. They describe how the encoded variables build an estimate conditional on that model; they are not separate physical energy uses or causal effects \citep{rudin2019,aas2021,fisher2019}.

\subsection{Short-context temporal models on irregular operational events}
The exploratory temporal comparison asks whether histories of completed same-line operations add information beyond the current-record covariates. Each eligible target receives the 16 most recent completed records from the same pelleting line and validation interval,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_i^{(16)}=\operatorname{last}_{16,(a_j,j)}
 \{j:\ell_j=\ell_i,\ t_j\in\mathcal P,\ a_j<t_i,\ a_j<\sup\mathcal P\},
 \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
where $a_j$ is the recorded completion time and ties are broken by source-row identifier. An operation that started earlier but was not complete at the target origin cannot enter the context.

The 16-record window gives every model the same short-context representation and retains 584 of the 800 development targets: 149 from Pellet~1, 257 from Pellet~2 and 178 from Pellet~3 over 21 origin dates. Contexts restart at the weekly validation boundaries so that the expanding blocks remain separated; completed outcomes may inform later targets within the same week but never update fitted parameters. Figure~\ref{fig:windows} shows the expanding design and one observed context.

\fig{07_windows}{windows}{1}{Short-context temporal evaluation. (a) Expanding training blocks and weekly validation blocks; green bars mark the outer validation weeks, and each training bar reports the earlier training / preceding-week validation counts used for epoch selection. (b) An observed context of 16 completed same-line operations for target row 823 on Pellet~1, ordered by completion; segments span recorded pelleting start and finish, dots mark completion, and every donor outcome precedes the target origin on 28 January at 04:24.}

Two Ridge controls separate historical information from model capacity. The first uses only the current-record covariates; the second flattens the current covariates, the 16 historical covariate vectors and their energy outcomes, keeping the penalty of 10. TFT and N-HiTS receive the same current and historical information through their NeuralForecast implementations \citep{nixtlatft,nixtlanhits}: TFT uses a hidden dimension of 32, four attention heads and dropout of 0.1, and N-HiTS uses three blocks with two 64-unit dense layers each, pooling factors of 2, 2 and 1 and interpolation downsampling factors of one. Each neural family uses seeds 11, 29 and 47, with an inner validation week immediately before each outer validation week. Optimization minimizes standardized-target MSE with Adam at a learning rate of $10^{-3}$, weight decay of $10^{-4}$, batch size 64 and gradient clipping at one, for at most 40 epochs with patience of eight; each seed is then refitted on the complete outer-training block for its internally selected number of epochs, and the three clipped predictions are averaged.

TimesFM~2.5 uses its frozen 200M checkpoint with energy-only input and with its native XReg adapter \citep{das2024,timesfm25,timesfmrepo}; XReg fits a local covariate regression for each origin from its 16 historical records and adds the frozen TimesFM residual forecast. Chronos-2 is evaluated with energy-only and native-covariate inputs, using its median forecast with cross-query learning disabled \citep{ansari2025,chronosrepo}. No foundation-model weights are updated locally. All temporal and control variants are evaluated on the same 584 targets, so the comparison concerns these specific short-context implementations on irregular pelleting events and is not a general ranking of the model families \citep{aksu2024,zeng2023}.

\subsection{Post-hoc exploratory extensions}
Three post-hoc extensions turn patterns seen during development into hypotheses for later periods. H1 lets the mass association vary by pelleting line, H2 lets it vary by product subfamily, and H3 corrects the frozen Ridge estimate with recent same-line residuals. For H1 and H2, with grouping variable $g_i$, the untruncated predictor is
\begin{equation}
 f_i=b+z_i^\top\beta+z_{m,i}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\gamma_c\ind(g_i=c),
 \label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
with the same Ridge penalty and transformations fitted on training data only. H3 uses up to 20 recent same-line residuals of the frozen model, each eligible after its operation's recorded completion and ordered by completion time and then by source-row identifier, which gives a deterministic rule for tied completions. With $e_j=y_j-\widehat y_j^{\mathrm{Ridge}}$ and $n_i=|\mathcal H_i|$,
\begin{equation}
 \delta_i=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{n_i}{n_i+10}\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{j\in\mathcal H_i}e_j,&n_i\ge5,\\
 0,&n_i<5,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \widehat y_i^{\mathrm{H3}}=\max(0,\widehat y_i^{\mathrm{Ridge}}+\delta_i).
 \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
H1--H3 are exploratory development analyses: they generate candidate refinements for future chronological evaluation and do not replace the frozen model.

\subsection{Empirically calibrated residual intervals}
Absolute errors from the 380 calibration records calibrate residual intervals for the frozen model. For miscoverage level $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
 q_\alpha=r_{(\lceil(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)},\qquad
 r_j=|y_j-\widehat y_j|,\qquad
 I_i^{1-\alpha}=[\max(0,\widehat y_i-q_\alpha),\widehat y_i+q_\alpha].
 \label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
The 90\% and 95\% half-widths are 93.01 and 132.96~kWh. The intervals are judged empirically by observed coverage, mean width and interval score, globally and by line; they are called residual intervals because the chronological industrial sequence is not assumed to be exchangeable. A post-hoc rolling extension updates the quantile from the latest 200 available absolute errors, starting from the calibration residuals and then admitting completed test outcomes while the point-prediction model stays frozen. Every donor satisfies
\begin{equation}
 a_j<t_i,\qquad a_j=\text{recorded pelleting completion of donor }j.
 \label{eq:arrival}
\end{equation}
The rolling calculation is an exploratory adaptation comparator, not an independently validated improvement \citep{xu2021,zaffran2022,gibbs2024}.

\subsection{Performance metrics and statistical analysis}
Point performance is measured by
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{MAE}&=N^{-1}\sum_i|\widehat y_i-y_i|,&
 \mathrm{RMSE}&=\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i(\widehat y_i-y_i)^2},\\
 \mathrm{Bias}&=N^{-1}\sum_i(\widehat y_i-y_i),&
 \mathrm{WAPE}&=\frac{\sum_i|\widehat y_i-y_i|}{\sum_i y_i}.
\end{align}
MAE is the primary selection criterion; RMSE emphasizes larger errors, bias quantifies systematic over- or underestimation, and WAPE expresses total absolute error relative to recorded energy \citep{hyndman2006}. Results are also reported by pelleting line and chronological period. Residual intervals are assessed by observed coverage, mean width and the interval score \citep{gneiting2007},
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{IS}_\alpha(L,U;y)=(U-L)+\frac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)\ind(y<L)
                  +\frac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)\ind(y>U).
 \label{eq:is}
\end{equation}
After an operation completes, its signed excess and interval flag are
\begin{equation}
 r_i^{\mathrm{excess}}=y_i-\widehat y_i,\qquad
 B_i=\ind\{y_i<L_i\;\text{or}\;y_i>U_i\}.
 \label{eq:outputs}
\end{equation}
These quantities support record-level review; they are statistical indicators and do not by themselves identify the physical cause of a deviation.

Pairwise differences use a circular block bootstrap with blocks of three observed origin dates, keeping all lines recorded on the same date in the same block, which preserves within-date dependence and short-range temporal grouping: 3,000 replicates for the primary test comparison and 5,000 for the exploratory comparisons. Alongside marginal 95\% intervals, H1--H3 use approximate Bonferroni-adjusted 98.33\% intervals and the seven temporal contrasts 99.29\% intervals; the adjustments apply to the stated comparisons within each analysis family. Line- and week-specific results are reported with their sample sizes as stratified assessments.

\subsection{Computational implementation}
Ridge and the native JAX XReg solver ran on CPU. Neural computations used float32 precision on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell GPU with 48~GB of memory, with TF32 disabled. TFT and N-HiTS were trained locally, whereas TimesFM and Chronos weights remained frozen. CPU--GPU numerical comparisons are computational diagnostics and are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:computing}.
