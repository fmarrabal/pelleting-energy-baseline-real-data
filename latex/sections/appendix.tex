\section{Computational and numerical reproducibility}\label{sec:computing}
The GPU experiment used Python 3.13.13, PyTorch 2.14.0 with CUDA 13.0, NeuralForecast 3.2.2, TimesFM package 3.0.2, Chronos-forecasting 2.3.2 and scikit-learn 1.9.0. The pretrained checkpoint revisions were \texttt{1d952420fba87f3c6dee4f240de0f1a0fbc790e3} for TimesFM 2.5 and \texttt{29ec3766d36d6f73f0696f85560a422f50e8498c} for Chronos-2. TimesFM received 16 observed context records with padding to a maximum context of 32 and a configured maximum horizon of 32; only its first forecast position was evaluated. Chronos used float32, disabled cross-query learning and returned the median forecast. Model configurations, checkpoints and record-level outputs are retained locally; pretrained weights are identified by revision rather than redistributed with the manuscript.

All 128,896 logged donor relationships passed checks for same-machine membership, partition membership and completion before the target origin. This total includes repeated relationships across nested and expanding blocks. Source-data, frozen-model and final-prediction fingerprints remained unchanged, and the shared CPU and GPU data, window and encoding functions were checked for consistency. These checks concern the recorded completion-time proxy; actual publication delays are not available in the source.

Within the GPU run, the 24 saved neural checkpoints were reloaded and compared with their stored inference, alongside four Chronos covariate batch-size checks. All 28 checks passed the recorded tolerances, with a maximum difference of $7.63\times10^{-5}$ kWh. Fresh CPU and GPU neural fits were less closely aligned, as optimization can follow different trajectories even with the same integer seed. TFT's CPU and GPU MAEs were 53.25 and 53.65 kWh; neither result established an advantage over the matched Ridge control under the paired intervals. Figure~\ref{fig:numerics} shows all three GPU seeds and their prediction ensemble.

For frozen Chronos-2 with covariates, the median absolute CPU--GPU prediction difference was 0.86 kWh and the maximum was 57.04 kWh. Its overall MAE changed from 85.65 to 85.53 kWh. Maximum prediction differences for both frozen TimesFM variants and univariate Chronos remained below $1.9\times10^{-4}$ kWh. To investigate the covariate sensitivity, the installed Chronos preprocessing and instance normalization were applied to the same 584 contexts, giving 6,424 target/covariate channels. Among 1,097 constant historical channels, 694 had a CPU--GPU difference above 0.1 in normalized context values; no non-constant channel exceeded that threshold. The largest difference was 0.88137 in the transformed coordinates.

The implementation substitutes a small scale only when the calculated scale is exactly zero. Tiny round-off variances in constant channels can consequently be amplified before the inverse-hyperbolic-sine transform. This provides a mechanism consistent with the numerical sensitivity, although it is not an end-to-end causal demonstration for each prediction. The reported benchmark retains the native preprocessing and original outputs; a corrective normalization policy would require a separately selected and evaluated experiment.
