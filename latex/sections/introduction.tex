\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Two pelleting operations can use very different amounts of electricity for entirely ordinary reasons: one processes more material, runs a different product, or takes place on another line. When a plant asks whether an operation used more energy than it should have, those differences must be accounted for before anything can be said about efficiency. The usual answer in feed manufacturing is a specific energy consumption (SEC) in kWh/t, which is simple and interpretable but cannot separate the effects of product mix, line assignment, processed mass and batch structure. Energy management standards make the alternative explicit: ISO 50001 asks for energy performance to be judged against a baseline normalized for the relevant variables, and ISO 50006 and the IPMVP describe how such baselines and performance indicators are built and used \citep{iso50001,iso50006,ipmvp2022}. Electricity is also a large share of the sector's energy bill: food processing in the United States consumes about 75~TWh of electricity, and electricity accounts for roughly 30\% of the reported energy use of UK food and beverage manufacturing \citep{compton2018,foster2023,park2021}.

Pelleting is a good place to build such a baseline, because its demand is strongly conditioned by what is processed. Pellet quality, conditioning, feed composition, steam application, temperature, particle characteristics and operating conditions all shape pelleting behaviour and mechanical energy demand \citep{thomas1996,thomas1997,thomas2020,skoch1981,bastiaansen2023}. Yet those studies aim at material properties, process behaviour or pellet quality; none builds and tests, chronologically, an operation-level electricity baseline from real plant records. That is the gap this paper addresses, and the next subsection places it against the closest published work.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Industrial energy baselines and performance indicators} Regression-based baselines for measurement and verification go back at least to the monthly-data models of Kissock and Eger \citep{kissock2008}, and automated baselines on interval data showed that predictive accuracy and honest uncertainty decide whether a saving can be claimed \citep{granderson2016,gallagher2018}. Key-performance-indicator frameworks translate the same idea to production facilities \citep{cosgrove2018}. In the feed industry itself, S\'anchez et al.\ \citep{sanchez2020} built motor-level efficiency assessments, energy baselines and performance indicators for a balanced-feed plant (about 10~kWh/t at 500~t/day, with an estimated saving potential of 364,000~kWh/year), but at an aggregated process level rather than per operation. Wigger et al.\ \citep{wigger2025} showed how AI models can be integrated into the energy management system of a manufacturing company from existing data resources, with consumption forecasting and anomaly detection as the use cases; their object is the company's consumption series, not the individual operation and what it processed.

\paragraph{Data-driven models of process energy} Regularized regression gives stable, interpretable estimates when predictors are correlated \citep{hoerl1970}, and tree ensembles and neural networks capture nonlinearities and interactions \citep{friedman2001}. Closest to our setting, Ekwaro-Osire et al.\ \citep{ekwaro2025} compared linear regression, tree-based methods and a multilayer perceptron on about 1,100 production batches with 67 input characteristics from an industrial compound-feed pelleting process and found that more complex models did not necessarily reduce error. That study relied on shuffled cross-validation, so it could not say how the models behave on a later period, and it did not consider operating histories, pretrained temporal models, predictive uncertainty or record-level traceability. Panjapornpon et al.\ \citep{panjapornpon2023} predicted the energy efficiency of a petrochemical process with an explainable transfer-learning LSTM made robust to sensor faults, and Mohammed et al.\ \citep{mohammed2025} combined multilinear regression with neural components for short-term load forecasting; both work on regularly sampled signals rather than on heterogeneous completed operations.

\paragraph{Temporal and foundation models} The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) \citep{lim2021} and N-HiTS \citep{challu2023} are the reference neural forecasters with covariates, and foundation models such as TimesFM and Chronos transfer representations learned from large time-series collections to new tasks \citep{das2024,ansari2024}. Benchmarks in this journal have begun to map where they help: the FETS benchmark evaluates foundation models against task-specific learners across 54 energy datasets \citep{obermeier2026}; zero-shot Chronos and TimesFM variants have been compared with tuned LSTMs and conventional models for electricity prices \citep{miseta2026,lin2026}; TimesFM has been assessed for battery degradation across datasets \citep{zhang2026battery}; and decision-focused fine-tuning has been proposed for feeder optimization \citep{beichter2025}. Comparative frameworks also stress the evaluation design itself (fixed-origin, expanding and rolling windows against linear and naive baselines \citep{micallef2025}; context length and horizon effects \citep{kavoosighafi2026}; transfer and zero-shot strategies \citep{neubauer2026}). All of these operate on regularly sampled series. Industrial pelleting records are different: consecutive completed operations have different masses, products, lines, start times and durations, so the prediction origin and the information available at that moment must be fixed before any model can be compared \citep{hewamalage2023}, and random or shuffled partitions inflate apparent performance \citep{tashman2000,cerqueira2020,bergmeir2018}.

\paragraph{Explainability and uncertainty} Explainable AI for energy systems has a growing literature \citep{machlev2022,saarela2024,cacao2025}, from frameworks that validate what data-driven power-curve models have learned \citep{letzgus2024} and sparse models that recover governing relations from plant data \citep{zhou2024nox}, to explainable drift detection in consumption forecasts \citep{samarajeewa2024}. Prediction intervals complete the picture: conformal methods have been compared for electricity prices and solar power \citep{oconnor2025,nam2025}, and their guarantees are known to weaken under temporal dependence and drift \citep{angelopoulos2021,barber2023,barber2021}; coverage must therefore be read together with width and checked by subgroup \citep{gneitingcal2007,gneiting2007}. Digital twins, finally, are the natural destination of a validated baseline \citep{tao2019,fu2026}, provided the statistical component is trustworthy.

No previous work combines real operation-level records, chronological validation with a frozen test, an exactly decomposable baseline, residual-calibrated intervals checked by line and a matched comparison with temporal and foundation models.

\subsection{Objective and contributions}\label{sec:contrib}
The objective of this study is to establish, on real operational records of three industrial feed-pelleting lines, whether an explainable, production-adjusted electricity baseline predicts the energy of a completed operation better than the line-specific SEC reference used in practice, and whether histories of completed operations, modern temporal models or pretrained foundation models add anything to it. Three things are new:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A chronologically validated, production-adjusted baseline for heterogeneous operations.} Seven estimators are compared on 800 chronological development targets, the selected Ridge regression is frozen with its interval calibration, and it is evaluated once on 798 later records against line-specific SEC. Previous feed-industry baselines are aggregated \citep{sanchez2020} or validated by shuffling \citep{ekwaro2025}.
\item \textbf{A matched test of operating histories, temporal models and foundation models on irregular industrial records.} TFT, N-HiTS, TimesFM 2.5 and Chronos-2 receive exactly the same 16 completed same-line operations and the same current descriptors as the linear control, on 584 identical targets, with the information available at the origin fixed in advance. Existing foundation-model benchmarks concern regularly sampled series \citep{obermeier2026,miseta2026,lin2026}.
\item \textbf{Record-level explainability and calibrated uncertainty that survive the chronological test.} Every estimate decomposes exactly into production, product, line and calendar terms, and residual-calibrated 90\% and 95\% intervals are assessed by line and by week, which is what distinguishes an unusual operation from a product mix.
\end{enumerate}
The validated scope is retrospective assessment at the closure of recorded operations; prospective monitoring is a later application that requires confirming predictor availability at the intended origin. For a plant, the result is a baseline that separates the ordinary variability of electricity use from deviations worth a look, and that can serve as the statistical component of an energy-management platform or digital twin.
