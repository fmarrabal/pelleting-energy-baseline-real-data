\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Production variability across lines and periods}
Figure~\ref{fig:production} summarizes production and electricity on the three lines: panels (a)--(c) contain the 2,742 records with positive pelleting energy, and panels (d)--(f) describe the mass, dosing-batch and product-family distributions of all 2,745 source records. Electricity generally rises with dosed mass, yet records of similar mass remain widely dispersed, and the distributions of mass, batch count and product family differ among lines and chronological partitions. Both observations argue for a production-adjusted baseline rather than a single plant-wide intensity value. Line-level differences in absolute or specific consumption must be read together with the corresponding production allocation; they are not controlled comparisons and say nothing about intrinsic mechanical efficiency.

\fig{02_production_and_energy}{production}{1}{Production and electricity across pelleting lines. (a--c) Dosed mass and pelleting electricity for the 2,742 positive-energy records, with line-specific medians annotated. (d--f) Mass distributions, dosing-batch frequencies and product-family mix of all 2,745 source records; values inside the product bars are record counts. Points are individual operations and are not uniformly spaced in time.}

\subsection{Baseline selection and frozen chronological test}
Table~\ref{tab:development} gives the development results of the seven candidates on the same 800 records. Ridge and histogram gradient boosting were practically tied at 51.48 and 51.50~kWh MAE, with ExtraTrees close behind at 52.31~kWh; the MLP and the three references had larger errors, and the recent-SEC estimator, although updated with completed validation outcomes, did worse than the static line-specific SEC. Ridge was selected for comparable accuracy, lower structural complexity and the availability of an exact additive decomposition, not because it was shown to be more accurate than gradient boosting.

The selected specification was fitted on the 1,542 eligible development records and frozen before the 798-record chronological test. Ridge reduced test MAE from 61.29 to 52.45~kWh (Table~\ref{tab:test}), a paired reduction of 8.84~kWh per record or 14.42\% with a 95\% block-bootstrap interval of [6.08, 11.50]~kWh; RMSE fell from 92.05 to 80.42~kWh and global WAPE from 22.34\% to 19.11\%. These percentages are reductions in prediction error, not in plant electricity consumption. Removing the five test records longer than 24 hours gave 52.32~kWh for Ridge and 61.15~kWh for SEC, so those records do not explain the advantage.

The production-adjusted model had lower MAE than SEC on every line (Figure~\ref{fig:testscatter}). Performance was most consistent on Pellet~2 and Pellet~3, with Ridge MAE of 43.36 and 53.36~kWh. Pellet~1 also benefited, its MAE falling from 72.31 to 66.37~kWh and its bias from $-64.03$ to $-53.85$~kWh, but the remaining systematic underestimation means that the frozen model was not operationally calibrated for that line during the test period. This localized shortfall does not invalidate the baseline or the gains on the other lines; it does mean that a common alerting criterion should not be applied across lines without line-specific calibration and chronological validation.

\subsection{Predictive value of completed operating histories}
The short-context comparison uses the 584 development records for which 16 completed same-line operations were available within the weekly interval; the other 216 of the original 800 did not meet the requirement, so these results apply to the matched population and are not comparable with the 800-record ranking. Table~\ref{tab:gpu} separates the Ridge controls, the locally trained temporal models and the frozen pretrained models.

Current-input Ridge reached 53.55~kWh MAE and TFT 53.65~kWh. The paired difference (Ridge minus TFT) was $-0.11$~kWh with a 95\% interval of [$-2.20$, 2.32]~kWh, which includes effects in both directions and gives no support for an improvement from TFT in the tested configuration. Giving Ridge the full 16-record history raised its MAE to 63.95~kWh, and N-HiTS reached 65.28~kWh. Chronos-2 did better with covariates than with energy alone, but both versions stayed well above the Ridge control, as did the energy-only TimesFM variant (Figure~\ref{fig:gpucomparison}).

\fig{08_gpu_comparison}{gpucomparison}{1}{Short-context comparison on the 584 matched development records. (a) MAE with 95\% block-bootstrap intervals. (b) Paired MAE reduction relative to current-input Ridge, so positive values favour the alternative; pale intervals are adjusted for seven comparisons and dark intervals are unadjusted 95\% intervals. The wide TimesFM XReg interval is shown in full.}

The TimesFM XReg configuration produced three extreme Pellet~3 predictions of about 2,753, 9,239 and 12,203~kWh for observed values of 185.4, 142.4 and 70.9~kWh. In those contexts dosed mass had almost no historical variability, so the standardized current mass extrapolated far outside the local range; an independent float64 reconstruction reproduced the behaviour, and the three predictions are retained in the official metrics. The diagnosis concerns numerical instability of this short-context XReg pipeline and says nothing about TimesFM with longer contexts or other scaling and regularization policies. Under the tested 16-record, same-line, within-week representation, historical inputs did not improve prediction over current-record covariates. This does not imply that operating histories lack value under other context definitions, longer observation periods or prospectively selected temporal models.

\subsection{Additive decomposition of the fitted predictor}
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} shows the operation-level output of the baseline: panel (a) the transformation of recorded production characteristics into an expected electricity value, a residual interval and a signed deviation, and panel (b) these outputs for source row 2080, a Pellet~2 operation with 18.002~t distributed over five dosing batches. The model estimated 255.90~kWh; the recorded value was 267.30~kWh, a residual of 11.40~kWh inside the fixed 95\% interval of [122.94, 388.85]~kWh. The record was chosen because its observed energy is close to the median test value; it does not represent the whole error distribution.

\fig{00_framework}{framework}{1}{Production-adjusted baseline output. (a) Recorded production conditions are transformed into an expected electricity value, residual intervals and a signed deviation; residuals from the later calibration period set the interval limits. (b) A real Pellet~2 operation (source row 2080): the light segment is the fixed 95\% interval, the dark segment the 90\% interval, the circle the estimate and the diamond the observed energy. An excess above the estimate is a model residual, not a measured saving or a confirmed fault.}

Applying Equation~\ref{eq:explain} to every frozen estimate reproduced all 798 archived test values to within $1.2\times10^{-13}$~kWh, and none required non-negative clipping. Figure~\ref{fig:explanation} separates the fitted coefficients from record-specific contributions: panel (a) gives the numerical coefficients in original input units, (b) the categorical coefficients under the fitted coding, (c) the distributions of the grouped contributions and (d) the decomposition of source row 2080, whose intercept and eight grouped contributions sum exactly to 255.90~kWh. After undoing the standardization used in fitting, the mass coefficient is 13.30~kWh per additional tonne and the dosing-batch coefficient 9.52~kWh per additional batch, conditional on the other encoded predictors.

\fig{11_explanation}{explanation}{1}{Additive decomposition of the frozen Ridge predictor. (a) Numerical coefficients in original input units. (b) All 16 categorical coefficients under the fitted coding. (c) Grouped contributions for every test record, with medians in red and interquartile ranges in orange. (d) Arithmetic decomposition of source row 2080. The reference combines standardized numerical means with all-zero categorical indicators and need not describe a feasible operating condition; contributions are conditional model terms, not causal effects.}

These values are coefficients of a fitted linear predictor, not separately identified physical requirements. Mass and batch count are correlated, and line and product allocation were not randomized, so their signs and magnitudes describe conditional associations. The intercept of 288.09~kWh is the algebraic reference of the fitted coding, not a standby consumption; categorical coefficients depend on the one-hot representation and its reference configuration; and small line coefficients do not demonstrate equal intrinsic efficiency among the lines. What the exact equality does provide is record-level traceability, without assigning causal meaning or physically separable energy uses to individual terms.

\subsection{Post-hoc exploratory extensions}
Three post-hoc extensions were evaluated on the 800 development records (Table~\ref{tab:explore}). Letting the mass association vary by product subfamily reduced MAE by 1.44~kWh (2.80\%) and the recent-residual correction by 1.71~kWh (3.33\%), both with multiplicity-adjusted intervals above zero; the line-dependent mass interaction reduced MAE by only 0.22~kWh, with an interval crossing zero. The gains varied across lines and validation weeks. Because H1--H3 were motivated by patterns observed during development, they are hypothesis-generating and need evaluation in a new chronological period; they do not replace the specification frozen for the primary test.

\subsection{Residual intervals and records flagged for review}
Figure~\ref{fig:alltest} shows the frozen estimates and fixed 95\% intervals for every test record, in chronological order within each line. The fixed 90\% and 95\% intervals covered 85.96\% and 93.23\% of the 798 records with mean widths of 183.63 and 258.85~kWh; their block-bootstrap 95\% coverage intervals, [81.86, 90.24]\% and [90.53, 95.87]\%, make global coverage broadly compatible with the nominal levels. Line-specific coverage differed: at the nominal 95\% level it was 90.00\% on Pellet~1 (20 records outside the interval), 96.40\% on Pellet~2 (12) and 91.70\% on Pellet~3 (22).

\fig{05_all_test_records}{alltest}{1}{Frozen Ridge estimates and fixed 95\% residual intervals for every test record, in chronological order within each pelleting line. Black points are observed electricity, coloured points the frozen estimate and vertical lines the intervals; red circles mark the 54 outcomes outside their intervals. Horizontal position is record order, so equal spacing does not imply equal elapsed time.}

In total 54 records fell outside the fixed 95\% intervals, 45 above the upper limit and nine below the lower one: about 6.77 flagged records per 100 operations, 5.64 upper and 1.13 lower exceedances. They are candidates for technical review because the calibrated baseline did not represent their energy well; without independent plant evidence they should not be classed as confirmed anomalies, inefficient operations, equipment faults or avoidable consumption.

The post-hoc rolling policy, which updates the quantiles from the most recent 200 completed residuals, raised coverage to 89.47\% and 94.49\% but widened the intervals by 19.66\% and 18.85\% while improving the interval score by only 1.12\% and 0.23\% (Table~\ref{tab:intervals}); most of the additional coverage came from width. The number of records outside the 95\% interval fell from 54 to 44. Because the policy was defined after the test results had been inspected and was not evaluated on an independent period, it is a calibration diagnostic, not a validated replacement for the fixed intervals.
